7 Things to Check Before You Buy a Daily Wellness Powder

Most powders look alike on the front of the pack. These seven checks show you what’s really different, and number 4 is the one almost nobody talks about.

If you’re comparing greens or wellness powders, or you already have a cupboard of half-finished tubs, this guide is for you. It takes four minutes and works on any brand.

Start with the label, not the marketing. The most useful question isn’t which powder has the longest list or the loudest promise. It’s whether you can see exactly what you’re getting, what it costs per day, and whether you’ll actually keep taking it.

01

A longer ingredient list doesn’t mean more is inside

Many powders group dozens of ingredients into a “proprietary blend” with a single total weight. You can see what’s in it, but not how much of each ingredient you’re getting.

A list of 70 names tells you very little if most of them are present in tiny, undisclosed amounts.

The check

Look for an amount next to every ingredient, not one total for the whole blend.

02

The front of the pack isn’t the label

The front is where the counts, colours and promises live. The back is where the facts live: the serving size and the amount of each ingredient per serving.

In the UK, health claims for vitamins and minerals must use specific authorised wording. Anything vaguer, like “boosts” or “supercharges”, is marketing language, not a claim you can compare.

The check

Turn the pack over. Compare per-serving amounts, then compare claims word for word.

03

The headline price isn’t the price

Work out the real daily cost: the price divided by the number of servings, plus delivery. Then compare like with like: one-off with one-off, subscription with subscription.

Watch for discounts that only apply to the first order, and check what the second delivery will cost.

04

The best formula is the one you actually take every day

This is the check almost nobody makes. In a well-known study from University College London, it took around 66 days on average for a new daily action to become automatic, and anything from 18 to 254 days depending on the person.

Every extra bottle, timing or instruction is another chance to skip a day. So a routine with six products and three different timings is harder to keep than it looks on the shelf.

Count the steps your routine asks of you, not just the ingredients. This is research about habits, not about any particular supplement.1

05

It has to fit an ordinary morning

Look for clear preparation instructions, no special equipment, and a format you can repeat on a rushed Tuesday, not just on a good day.

If taste matters to you, check how it’s flavoured and sweetened before you buy, rather than relying on adjectives.

06

Terms and guarantee should be easy to find

Before you subscribe, check when it renews, how to pause or cancel, whether any gift is a one-off, and how long the money-back guarantee lasts.

Also check there’s a real person to contact if something goes wrong. Clear terms are part of what you’re paying for.
